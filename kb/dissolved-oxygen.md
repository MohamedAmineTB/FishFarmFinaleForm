## The daily oxygen cycle
In ponds and tanks with algae, oxygen follows the sun. Algae make oxygen by photosynthesis during the day, so dissolved oxygen (DO) climbs through the morning and peaks in mid-afternoon. At night photosynthesis stops, but algae, fish and bacteria keep using oxygen, so DO falls all night and reaches its lowest point just before dawn. The pre-dawn low is the moment to watch: most oxygen-related fish kills happen between about 3 a.m. and sunrise.

## Safe oxygen levels for warm-water fish
Most warm-water fish do best when DO stays at 5 mg/L or more. Fish feed less and grow more slowly below that level. Emergency aeration is recommended when DO drops below about 4 mg/L or when fish gasp at the surface, and fish start to die when it stays below about 2 mg/L. Cold-water fish such as trout need more oxygen than warm-water fish.

## What makes a night oxygen dip worse
Cloudy days (less photosynthesis), warm water above about 27 °C (warm water holds less oxygen and fish breathe faster), heavy evening feeding, dense algae blooms and sudden algae die-offs all deepen the pre-dawn low. A fast oxygen decline in the evening is an early warning sign of a low night.
